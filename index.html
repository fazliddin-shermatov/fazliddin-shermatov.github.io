Research Interests

Economics of Science & Innovation AI & High-Performance Computing Innovation Economics Firm Performance Medical Devices Knowledge Collaboration Patents & Bibliometrics Econometrics

My research traces the full chain from science to economic outcomes: how AI itself emerges from enabling technologies and computing infrastructure, how it diffuses into scientific discovery and firm-level innovation, and how it ultimately translates into market performance — Science → AI → Innovation → Performance. My work on AI and high-performance computing examines the first half of this chain, showing that the computational synergy between the two is most powerful precisely at the scientific frontier. My work on the medical device industry examines the second half, tracing how AI research collaboration and AI-enabled product innovation feed through to firm-level productivity and performance. Empirically, this means combining administrative microdata — company products, patent records, bibliometric indices, and firm-level financial data — with structural econometric methods to identify causal effects of AI-driven innovation on firm performance.

Working Papers

2026 Work in Progress

AI Innovation and Firm Performance in the Medical Device Industry

Fazliddin Shermatov, Stéphane Robin, Aldo Geuna

An AI-enabled medical device must obtain regulatory clearance before reaching a patient, leaving a rare, dated, firm-attributable record of AI innovation output. We exploit this setting with a three-stage recursive model estimated on a novel firm-level dataset linking FDA premarket clearances, USPTO patents, Scopus publications, and Orbis financials, tracing the innovation chain from external AI research collaboration through AI device introduction to firm performance. External collaboration robustly drives AI device introduction, with a larger effect for small firms; device introduction in turn raises labour productivity robustly, while effects on profit margins are weaker and less stable, consistent with competitive entry eroding pricing power as AI devices diffuse through the sector.

Publications

2026 Published

Scientific Discovery in the Age of AI and Supercomputing

Stefano Bianchini, Aldo Geuna, Fazliddin Shermatov

Scientific Reports (2026)

DOI ↗
Drawing on metadata from more than five million scientific publications (2000–2024) across 27 fields, we examine how the convergence of AI and high-performance computing relates to scientific breakthroughs. The synergy between the two is strongest at the research frontier: work combining AI and HPC is far more likely to introduce novel ideas and reach top-cited status than conventional research or work using either capability alone — in some fields, AI+HPC papers are several times more likely to land among the top 1% most cited, and the predicted likelihood of introducing a genuinely novel term roughly triples relative to work using neither technology. We also document growing disparities in access to supercomputing and AI expertise, increasingly concentrated in a small number of regions led by the US and China, with the EU27 remaining competitive in combined AI+HPC output.
